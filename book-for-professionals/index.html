What changes

What will this book change for me?

It won't give you a framework to follow. It gives you a mindset and a handful of ideas to act on, inside a company or on your own.

Mindset over framework

Innovation is a state of mind more than a process. Stop waiting for the perfect method and start with purpose.

Read the incentives

Game theory explains why good ideas die inside bad structures, and how sound incentives make people work together.

Handle the unavoidables

Uncertainty, getting stuck and failure will happen. The book shows how to move anyway, and how to get unstuck.

Commit or do nothing

Half measures rarely lead to breakthroughs. Learn to say no, so you can say a full yes to what matters.

Spot your moment of truth

Recognize the decision that can change your path, and be ready before it arrives.

Work with purpose

Eudaimonia: success that also brings fulfillment, to you and to the people around you.

Questions professionals ask

Is Unstable Innovation only for entrepreneurs?

No. It is for anyone who feels the urge to build something and add value, including employees who push new ideas inside a company. Employee or entrepreneur, the mindset is the same.

Is it a business book?

Partly. It covers incentives, business models and megatrends, but at heart it is, in Nelson's own words, a philosophy book disguised as an innovation and entrepreneurship book.

How is it different from other innovation books?

Most innovation books sell a framework. This one argues that breakthroughs come from mindset, timing and commitment, not from a step-by-step method, and it backs that up with real stories from large companies and startups.

I'm in my 30s or 40s. Is it too late for this book?

No. The more experience you bring, the more of the philosophy under each chapter you will see: Stoicism, incentives, and what a good life really means.

How long is it?

215 pages in 9 chapters, about 3 to 4 hours of reading. Plain language, no jargon for the sake of it.
