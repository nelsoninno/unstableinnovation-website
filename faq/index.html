FAQ

What is Unstable Innovation about?

It is a book about innovation, entrepreneurship, and philosophy. It teaches how to embrace chaos, uncertainty, and acceleration as the path to real innovation. Nelson draws on Stoicism, game theory, megatrends, and his career across aviation, finance, education, and Bitcoin.

Who is this book for?

People who question everything and want to build or change something: curious teenagers from 13, young adults figuring out their next step, and professionals who feel the urge to create, whether they are employees or entrepreneurs. It is not a book of theory or a recipe for success. It is a book about figuring yourself out. See who should read it, and who should not.

Who should not read Unstable Innovation?

People looking for a step-by-step recipe for success, an academic textbook or a get-rich-quick plan. It is also not meant for children under 13, since most of the ideas need more life experience to land. More on who should skip it.

Is the book really free? Why?

Yes. Nelson wants the ideas to reach as many people as possible, so the PDF, EPUB, and Kindle versions are free on this site, and all we ask is your first name. Your email is optional. The book is shared under a Creative Commons license (BY-NC-SA 4.0), so you can pass it on. Bitcoin donations via Geyser Fund are welcome, never required.

Who is Nelson Inno?

Nelson Inno is a Salvadoran entrepreneur, co-founder and CEO of WeSpark, TEDx speaker, and author of Unstable Innovation. He builds AI-powered education systems for large enterprises, governments, and universities, and co-designed Bitcoin Diploma 2.0 with El Salvador's National Bitcoin Office, a curriculum the Ministry of Education plans to bring to up to 500,000 students. His official personal website is nelsoninno.com.

Does Nelson work with enterprises or governments on AI, Bitcoin, or innovation projects?

Yes. Nelson works with innovation departments, digital-transformation teams, and technology teams inside large enterprises and governments on three areas: AI education, training, and implementation; Bitcoin education and implementation; and innovation and technology transformation. The fastest way to reach him is LinkedIn. It is the only channel he uses for engagement inquiries.

Is this book good for teenagers and young adults?

Yes. Unstable Innovation is a short, easy-to-read book that gives teenagers and young adults their first serious taste of philosophy, alongside concrete examples of ethics, resilience, and purpose. Parents give it to their kids as an early guide to why their actions matter, why bringing love and value to the world matters, and why effort is what builds a strong life. Read more in our guide for parents of teenagers and our page for young adults.

What will a young reader take from this book?

A clear message that their actions matter, that they can bring both love and value to the world, and that they build strong lives through effort. The book pairs a short, readable format with philosophy that lasts: when life gets hard, they will already know it is supposed to be hard, and they will have the tools to push forward and become greater people, with strong ethical and moral principles as their compass.

Does an official Spanish edition exist? Who translated it?

Yes. Innovación Inestable is the official Spanish edition. The translation was funded by the Gloria Kriete Foundation. All PDF, EPUB, and Kindle versions are also available in Spanish (see /es/get/), and the paperback ships from Amazon Mexico, Spain, and other Amazon marketplaces.

Is it a practical book on how to start new projects and be creative at work?

It gives short, concrete tools you can use right away: seven questions to test an idea with real people before you build it, killing your first idea, prototyping with AI, SCAMPER, the Design Sprint, the Business Model Canvas and eight skills every innovator needs. Each tool gets a short explanation, not a full workbook. The core of the book is the mindset behind starting new things, on your own or inside the company you work for. See Unstable Innovation for professionals.

I feel stuck in life. Can this book help?

It was written for people who feel the urge to build but get stuck. The chapters on uncertainty, getting stuck and failure share the practices Nelson uses when things go wrong, and the last chapters bring in Stoic philosophy (Epictetus, Seneca) to focus on what you can control and to define success for yourself. It is a book of ideas and simple practices, not a step-by-step self-help program.

Is Unstable Innovation a book about Bitcoin?

Partly. One chapter explains money, inflation and why incentives matter, with Bitcoin as the example of better incentives, and the idea of low time preference runs through the whole book. The book itself says it is not a Bitcoin book: Bitcoin sits next to Stoicism, megatrends and entrepreneurship.
