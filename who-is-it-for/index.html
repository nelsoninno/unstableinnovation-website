People who question everything, dream big and feel they have a role to play in a world that changes too fast. That includes curious teenagers from 13, young adults figuring out what's next, and professionals who feel the urge to build something. It is not a book of theory or a recipe for success. It is a book about figuring yourself out.

Better to know now

Who should not read Unstable Innovation?

Skip this book if you are looking for any of these. Better to find out now than after 215 pages.

  • A step-by-step recipe for success. There is no ten-step framework here. The book is about mindset, not a checklist.
  • An academic textbook on innovation management, with a model and a citation on every page.
  • A get-rich-quick plan. The book says it plainly: rewards are not guaranteed, and the ones that come usually come late.
  • Comfort. If you want to be told the world is fine as it is, this book will annoy you.
  • A novel. It is nonfiction: real stories, philosophy and ideas you can use.
  • A book for young children. Under 13, most of the ideas need more life experience to land.
Age guide

What age is Unstable Innovation for?

13 and up. The language is clean and the chapters are short, so a curious 13 year old can read it. The more life you have lived, the more of the philosophy under each chapter you will see.

Questions people ask

Who should read Unstable Innovation?

People who question everything and want to build or change something: curious teenagers from 13, young adults figuring out their next step, and professionals who feel the urge to create, whether they are employees or entrepreneurs.

Who should not read Unstable Innovation?

People looking for a step-by-step recipe for success, an academic textbook or a get-rich-quick plan. It is also not meant for children under 13.

What age is Unstable Innovation for?

13 and up. The language is clean and the chapters are short. Younger teens get the stories and the big ideas. Older readers see more of the philosophy under every chapter.

Do I need to want to be an entrepreneur to read it?

No. If you care about entrepreneurship, great. If you don't, that's also okay. The book is for anyone who wants to be more creative and add value to others, as a student, an employee or a founder.

Is Unstable Innovation a theory book?

No. It doesn't sell a recipe or ten steps to success. It focuses on figuring yourself out: when and where you should be to spark change, take control of your life and live it on your own terms.
